# RIALITI home page: second opinion

**Verdict: the founder is right.** The page is a well-made film trailer for a mission. It is not a product page. Nothing on it says what RIALITI Capture is, who uses it, or what you do with it.

## 1. Five-second test

**At the top.** A stranger lands on a black screen with "Every photograph is a promise: this happened." and the word "Scroll". There is no product name, no app, no verb, no button. The top-right tabs are hidden at load and only fade in with beat 1. A marketplace seller who came for "proof your listing photos are real" sees a poem. Those who stay see a toddler, Earthrise and a wedding, none of which look like their problem.

**Where it loses them.** The page loses them in the first viewport and does not try to win them back until about 12.75 screen heights later. (opening 100svh, five beats at 175svh, two at 150svh), on a phone, with no product word anywhere. Only the turn (beat 5) hints at a threat, and implicitly.

**At the end.** The closing says "To protect what's human", "Keep what's real, real", then "Get early access" (a mailto) and a sideload APK with an alpha warning. The visitor leaves with a cause, maybe an app, maybe unfinished. "Early access" contradicts the live Play listing. "RIALITI Check", the free thing anyone can use today, appears only as a small tab label with no explanation.

## 2. The core problem

It is all of it, but one cause drives the rest. **The page was written to a rule ("mechanism-free, poetic, never explain how") that made sense for a pre-product mission page on 2026-08-03 and is now wrong for a live app with paid traffic.** That rule removed the product name, the use cases, the three steps and the proof moment. The order follows: emotion first, offer last, twelve screens apart. The missing proof is the most costly gap. The "test it yourself" demo is already written, honest and styled, and it sits commented out. The page has a product that can prove itself in thirty seconds and never lets it.

## 3. Recommended architecture

The ads point straight at the Play listing, not here. Home visitors come from the listing's website link, name searches after an ad, check.rialiti.io and soon the guides. They arrive curious and want answers fast, so build the page as the hub.

**1. Hero (one viewport, static, no scroll needed).**
Job: name the product, say what it does, give both doors.
Headline and subline: see section 4. Under them, the Play badge and a text link "Check a photo for free". One real image: the app's Verified capture screen with record id `ra-e58e1b6d5bed4de9`.

**2. The problem, in one line and one picture.**
Job: give the threat one beat instead of five.
Headline: "Photos used to be proof. Now anyone can make one."
Body: "A listing photo, a damage photo, a photo of the person you're meeting. Looking real no longer means it is."

**3. What you can use it for.**
Job: let each visitor find themselves. Six one-line scenarios as an uneven list, not a card grid.
Headline: "For any photo that has to be believed."
Body: "Shoot it in RIALITI and send it anywhere. Anyone can check it."
- Selling a used car: "Show the real dents, the real mileage, today."
- Watches and phones: "Prove you have it in hand, not a photo you found."
- Shopify and marketplace sellers: "Product photos buyers can check for themselves."
- Renting out or moving out: "A dated record of the condition, before and after."
- Pets and rehoming: "Show the animal is real and really yours."
- Insurance, disputes, reporting: "A photo that can show it happened, unedited."
Each tile later links to its SEO guide, which is what makes this the hub.

**4. How it works, in three steps.**
Job: make it feel simple. Plain verbs, no mechanism words.
Headline: "Three steps. No account."
1. "Take the photo in the free RIALITI Capture app."
2. "Send it anywhere. WhatsApp, a listing, an email."
3. "Anyone checks it free at check.rialiti.io."
Body under the steps: "An edited photo or an AI image traces back to nothing."

**5. The proof moment: test it yourself.**
Job: earn trust by letting them try. Ship the dormant demo section with its three files, or a two-file version (original plus WhatsApp copy) if the AI recreation is not ready.
Headline: "Don't trust us. Check it yourself."
Body: "Download a real capture and its WhatsApp copy. Put both through RIALITI Check and read the answer."
Keep its fine print: RIALITI checks where a file came from, not whether it looks real.

**6. Why this exists (the photo story, cut down).**
Job: carry the mission for people who scroll this far.
Headline: "Some moments happen once."
Body: "We think real moments deserve proof. Verification will always be free."
**My recommendation: move the story down and cut it to three beats.** Keep beat 1 (first steps), beat 5 (the turn) and beat 7 (Blue Marble with the mission line). Those three hold the whole arc: real, then faked, then protected. The other four are beautiful but repeat the first, and each costs a phone screen. Three beats also ends the seven-letter rail; better to lose the rail than the visitor. Second best: all seven, unchanged, below section 5. Never above the product.

**7. Close.**
Job: one decision.
Headline: "Keep what's real, real."
Body: "Free on Android. Checking is free forever."
Play badge (replacing the APK block and its alpha notes), an iPhone waitlist field with "Not on iPhone yet. We'll tell you when it is.", then the mission line "To Protect What's Human".

**Footer:** RIALITI Check, Guides, Privacy, Photograph credits, founders@rialiti.io.

## 4. Three hero headlines

**A. "Prove your photo is real."**
Subline: "Take it in the free RIALITI Capture app. Anyone can check it, even after WhatsApp."
Plain, seller-first, matches the ad promise.

**B. "Looking real isn't proof anymore. This is."**
Subline: "RIALITI Capture gives every photo a public record anyone can check for free."
Carries the threat, but slower to parse.

**C. "Ask for proof before you pay."**
Subline: "Sellers shoot in RIALITI Capture. Buyers check the photo free in seconds."
Best buyer line, but installs are a seller act.

**Pick: A.** Five words, readable on a phone, true for every scenario in section 3. B is the A/B challenger.

## 5. What must not change, and risks

**Keep:** the canonical mark and wordmark; "To Protect What's Human" on the page; "Every photograph on this page is real." and "Verification will always be free."; real, credited photos only; the no-JS and reduced-motion complete page; WCAG AA contrast; no testimonials, logos, numbers or press that do not exist.

**Honesty rail.** Write "prove it's real" and "ask for proof". Never "spot fakes", "detect AI" or "AI detector". The v2 ad headline "Spot the edit." leans toward detection. The home page should not echo it. Use "Proof it's real. Not edited." instead.

**Risks.**
- Going generic. Hero, tiles and three steps is every SaaS page. The defence is real artifacts (the actual verified screen, record id, WhatsApp copy) and the photo story as the one bold section.
- Scope creep from the guides. The home page links to guides; it does not become one.
- The low-RAM acceptance phone failed twice before. Re-test any new story there.
- Brand drift. The site is dark with Lexend and Playfair, the ads are Inter on dark app tokens, and the campaign system is cream and forest. Pick one so the ad-to-site handoff feels like one brand.

**Fix on any version.** The beat 5 line and the page title carry em dashes; replace with commas. The "Get early access" mailto exposes a personal Gmail address on a public page. Move it to founders@rialiti.io or the waitlist form.

## 6. Questions only the founder can answer

1. Do you approve lifting the "mechanism-free, never explain how" rule for the home page, keeping it only for the story section?
2. The story: cut to three beats (my pick), keep all seven below the fold, or replace it?
3. May the page show the real record id and app screen, and may we publish a real capture plus its WhatsApp copy as downloadable demo files?
4. Which visual system leads: the current dark site, or the cream and forest campaign system?
5. iPhone waitlist: which form or backend collects it, and what do we promise about timing (I suggest no date)?
