How it works

Witness. Archive. Verify.

Three layers carry trust from the camera sensor to the person watching.

01 — WITNESS

Capture, attested in hardware

At the moment of recording, the phone's secure hardware (TEE) signs the capture with a key that never leaves the chip — validated against the manufacturer's root of trust. Not an app promise: silicon.

02 — ARCHIVE

A fingerprint that survives

The capture's perceptual fingerprint is written to an append-only transparency log — the same construction browsers use for TLS certificates — and anchored to a public log. It matches the footage even after platforms re-encode it.

03 — VERIFY

Free, public verdicts

Anyone drops a file on the verify page and gets an evidence-backed verdict in seconds, with the cryptographic proof one click away. Verification is free and public — always.

Verdicts

We say exactly what we can prove — and no more

Binary “real / fake” stamps die in public. Reality grades evidence honestly.

ATTESTED

This exact footage was signed inside a phone's secure hardware at capture time, and the file you're holding matches that recording.

REGISTERED

The footage's fingerprint is in the archive and we know who registered it — with no hardware proof of the camera. Weaker, and labeled as such.

UNKNOWN ≠ FAKE

Footage we've never seen is simply unknown. Absence of proof is not proof of fakery — a trust product that overclaims is worse than none.

Why now

Metadata gets stripped. Detection decays. Provenance survives.

Metadata-based provenance breaks in transit

Industry approaches attach signed metadata to the file — and most social platforms strip or re-encode it away before a viewer ever sees it. Reality's proof lives server-side, keyed to what the footage looks like, so it survives the trip.

Detectors age. Cryptography doesn't.

Every classifier that spots today's fakes trains tomorrow's generators. A hardware signature over a capture, anchored in an append-only public log, doesn't degrade as models improve.
